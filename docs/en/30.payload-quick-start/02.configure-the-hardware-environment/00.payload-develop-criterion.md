To ensure the safety of the drone when carrying payload devices to perform flight tasks, please follow DJI's payload device development standards when developing payload devices with PSDK.

## Hardware Port Standards
::: danger Note:

* **Do not** short-circuit the pins of the drone or hardware platform.
* **Do not** connect the drone to a power output system or input current to the aircraft.
* The payload capacitance must be ≤500uF. If it exceeds this threshold, the aircraft will trigger short-circuit protection at the moment of power-on, and the SDK power supply will shut off its output.
* The protection current is 4A. Once the current limit is exceeded, the aircraft will cut off the power within 5ms to protect flight safety.
:::

### High-Voltage Output Trigger Mechanism

::: warning Note:
This section applies only to the **Matrice 5TD**.
:::

After the payload port powers up, it outputs 5 V by default. The aircraft switches the port to high-voltage output (15 V-25.8 V) only after confirming that a valid third-party payload is connected. This process is completed automatically by the aircraft, and **developers do not need to apply for it**.

The aircraft identifies the payload in the following order:

1. **USB enumeration**: After detecting that a payload is connected, the aircraft waits for the payload to complete USB enumeration. If the payload can start up at 5 V and establish USB communication with the aircraft using the specified third-party payload enumeration information, the aircraft identifies it as a third-party payload and outputs high voltage. The time required for this path depends on how quickly the payload's USB becomes ready, and is usually shorter.
2. **SDK certified chip query**: If no USB enumeration is detected after approximately 6 s, the aircraft queries the SDK certified chip over I2C instead, and outputs high voltage once a valid chip is found. The query usually takes 100 ms to 2 s, and may take up to 12 s in extreme cases.

The Matrice 5TD must connect to payloads through the E-Port V3 coaxial cable, which has a built-in SDK certified chip. **Developers do not need to integrate a certified chip themselves**; they only need to complete <a href="../app-bind.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">App Binding</a>. If the payload cannot complete startup and USB enumeration at 5 V, the aircraft identifies it through the certified chip built into the coaxial cable and outputs high voltage.

### Power Supply Constraints

::: warning Note:
These constraints apply only to the **E2 and E3 low-speed ports** of the **Matrice 5TD**, and do not apply to other aircraft or ports.
:::

During power-up, the low-speed port supply voltage VBUS first stabilizes at 5V and then switches to the high-voltage level (20V). The payload must design the enable logic of its downstream power rails and loads according to this power-up and voltage-switching timing. To ensure proper operation and prevent component damage, third-party payloads must observe the following constraints when designing their power supply:

1. **Load capability at the 5V level**: At the 5V level, the low-speed port provides a load capability of **5V / 1A**. The payload's total current at this level must not exceed 1A. Drawing excessive current will trigger overcurrent protection, after which this port will no longer supply power for the current flight.
2. **Power-up timing of downstream components**: Downstream power rails and load components connected after the low-speed port's 5V (VBUS_5V) must wait at least **10 ms** after VBUS_5V has stabilized (i.e., the 5V level power-up is complete) before being enabled and starting to operate. Enabling downstream components too early may disrupt the payload's own power-up timing and affect normal operation.
3. **Load limitation during voltage switching**: During the transition of VBUS from 5V to 20V, high-power load components must not be enabled, and the total current at the low-speed port must be kept below 1A. High-power loads may only be enabled after the voltage switch is complete and 20V has stabilized.

::: danger Note:
Enabling high-power loads prematurely during voltage switching and causing the current to exceed 1A may affect the normal operation of the load. Any resulting issues are not covered by the warranty.
:::

#### Payload Power-up Timing

The figure below shows the payload power-up timing requirements. After VBUS_5V_20V has established 5V and stabilized, the MCU_3V3 and the low-power load (L_LOAD_EN) may be enabled only after a delay T1. After VBUS has switched from 5V to 20V and stabilized, the high-power load (H_LOAD_EN) may be enabled only after a delay T2. ## Structural Design Standards

::: tip Note:

* To prevent the payload device mounted on the drone from being affected by the drone's flight inertia and colliding with the battery compartment and propellers, which could damage the drone or the payload device, please design the dimensions of the payload device reasonably.
* To prevent the payload device from affecting the drone's moment of inertia, please minimize the dimensions of the payload device as much as possible.
:::
